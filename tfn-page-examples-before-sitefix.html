        01

        Why it works

        Every choice has a reason grounded in how people read, trust and decide. When something new comes up, decide it the same way.

        Principle

        Black and white, colour only in the product

        The structure stays quiet so the product can speak.

        How TFN does it

        Sections are white or black. Colour lives in things: the orbs, the app, the maps, the photos. Buttons and type stay ink or white.

        Why it works

        Processing fluency. Plain, high-contrast surfaces are easier to read, and what feels easy feels trustworthy. With money and fuel, calm reads as safe.

        Principle

        Textured gradients, not flat colour

        Soft colour fields with a fine grain, like light through frosted glass.

        How TFN does it

        Each product family has a matte orb: diesel blue, non-fuel, cross-border, Homebase. The same texture runs from the site to the map markers. Never behind text.

        Why it works

        Texture reads as material. A fine grain makes colour feel tangible and calm rather than digital and glaring, which signals care (the aesthetic-usability effect: what looks considered is trusted to work). It becomes a distinctive asset people recognise without the logo, and the grain stops gradients banding on screens.

        Principle

        One sans, tight and confident

        A single geometric grotesk with tight tracking at large sizes, and short headlines.

        How TFN does it

        One sans (Aeonik Pro, Satoshi until licensed). Headlines of three to six words. The serif is kept for the wordmark only.

        Why it works

        Cognitive load. One typeface and one weight per role means the eye never re-learns the page. Short lines get read; long ones get skimmed.

        Principle

        Pills mean action

        You can tell what is clickable from its shape alone.

        How TFN does it

        Every call to action is a full pill: ink on light, white on dark. In the app, the main action (Go, Start) is the blue-to-lavender gradient.

        Why it works

        Affordance. A consistent shape for "do something" lets people act without hunting. One primary action per view keeps the choice easy (Hick's law).

        Principle

        Show the product, prove with numbers

        Real screens carry the story; figures replace adjectives.

        How TFN does it

        The app, the portal and the map are the hero. Claims are numbers we can stand behind: 270+ truck stops, 44M+ litres a month, 168K+ transactions.

        Why it works

        Specificity and social proof. An exact number is believed more than "many". Volume from other transporters says "people like you already trust this".

        Principle

        Colour tells products apart

        Each product keeps one accent, everywhere.

        How TFN does it

        On-Road blue, LCV cyan, Non-Fuel slate, Network indigo, Homebase teal, Africa violet, Credit rose.

        Why it works

        Recognition over recall. When colour always means the same product, people stop reading labels and just know.

        Principle

        The logo never changes

        One wordmark, two colours, fixed space around it.

        How TFN does it

        The TFN wordmark is black on light or white on dark, nothing else, with clear space equal to the height of the T.

        Why it works

        Mere exposure. We come to like what we see repeatedly, but only if it looks the same each time. A logo that varies resets the clock.

        Two more, for a price-led product

        Price first

        On the map, the price is the marker. The cheapest in view says "Lowest". Savings are shown against the regional average.

        Anchoring. A price only means something next to a reference. The average is a fair anchor; the priciest stop would flatter us and erode trust.

        The odd one out

        Colour is used so rarely that when it appears it means something: the Lowest pill, your fuel stops, a warning spark.

        Von Restorff (isolation) effect. The one item that differs is the one remembered. Spend colour on what you want noticed.

        The idea

        Fuel management, sorted. Negotiated diesel at 270+ truck stops, the payment and paperwork handled, every litre live.

        Look and feel

        Premium fintech, built for transport. Upmarket and exact, never glossy, never cheap.

        The calm of a private bank, the clarity of a live dashboard, the honesty of the road. Restraint is the luxury: few colours, real photos, exact numbers.

        Who it's for

        Transport owners and their operations and fleet managers, who buy the fuel. Drivers, who use the app at the pump. Service providers running subcontractors.

        What we promise

        A lower price, no admin and no surprises. Every fill logged against the truck and driver before it pulls away.

        What's always true

        No cards, codes or slips for trucks. The QR at the till is for LCVs only. Credit comes through Standard Bank, Absa or Preference Capital.

        Personality

        Calm, exact and on your side. Like a good fleet manager: knows every number, never raises their voice.

        Orbs and gradients

        The orb is TFN's icon and its colour: deep, matte colour fields under a fine grain, like light through frosted glass at night. One orb per product family.

        Do and don't

        Why texture
        Grain makes colour feel like a material, calm rather than glaring, and stops gradients banding.
        Depth
        Deep tones over bright pastels. The orb glows on black; it never shouts on white.
        Where
        Inside things: orbs, product art, the app's one main action. Never behind body text or the wordmark.
        Coins
        Silver, real 3D, floating clear of the orbs.

        Style
        Outline only, one colour: ink, white or the accent.
        Sizes
        16, 20, 24px. Never stretched, never filled.
